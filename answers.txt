1. 
Two Pros of Agile Development
- uses regular iterations and stakeholder feedback, improving product quality by helping identify and solve issueas early
- agile development allows the users/teams to respond quickly to changes in requirements due to the iterative process
Two Cons of Agile Development
- timelines and budget may be harder to estimate because requirements may change through each iteration
- there needs to be high team commitment-- Agile inherently demands consistent communication and engagement, which could be hard for part-time or distributed teams

2. 
Scrum Master Roles & Responsibilities:
- essentially the coach & facilitator for the SCRUM team, focusing on efficiency
- For example, they:
   - facilitate Scrum events like sprint planning, review, and retrospectives
   - protect the team from external distractions
   - coach the team & stakeholders on Agile principles and practices
   - ensure the team follows Scrum processes

Product Owner Roles & Responsibilities:
- they represent the voice of the customer & defines + prioritizes the product backlog & general product direction
- To illustrate, they:
   - communicate the vision & goals of the product
   - create and manage the product backlog
   - ensure the development team understands the requirements & user needs
   - collaborate with stakeholders to gather & refine requirements
   - accept or reject work that was completed during a sprint based on defined criteria.

3.  Types of sprint cycle meetings

Sprint Planning Meetings
- they define the overarching objective for the upcoming sprint, ensuring it aligns with the project's overall goals
- the product backlog is reviewed. Items are prioritized and tasks are assigned to team members
Daily Scrum (stand up) Meetings
- team members discuss what they accomplished the previous day, what they plan on doing today, and any blockers/challenges they are facing
- obstacles or impediments (blockers) are discussed, and team members plan on how to address them
Sprint Review Meetings
- the development team presents the work completed during the sprint to stakeholders, showcasing potentially shippable incrementations of the product
- feedback is gathered from stakeholders on the work that is presented. this helps adjust priorities for the next sprint

4.
Epic:
A very high level view of a projects progress. Epics are large bodies of work that can be broken down into smaller tasks. They group together related stories, tasks, and bugs. An example would be "New User Interface for Web Application"
Story:
A specific requirement of piece of functionality, usually written from the user's perspective to capture user requirements. These are usually smaller than epics but help in understanding what needs to be built. An example would be "As a user, I want to be able to ---"
Bug:
A bug is an issue that negatively impacts the functionality of a software app. It's a problem that needs to be fixed. An example of a bug would be when a software application crashes when a specific button is clicked.
Task:
A task is just a piece of work that needs to be done. This can be related to a story, bug, or any other type. They just help in organizing and tracking progress. An example would be testing a new feature in a software app.
